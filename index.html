About

Engineering background, analytics practice

I studied Computer Engineering at the University of Jaffna, which is where the instinct to treat business problems as systems — inputs, transformations, feedback loops — comes from. That instinct now shows up in how I approach analytics: build the pipeline once, make it formula-transparent, and let the output stand up in front of decision-makers without a slide of caveats.

Day to day, I work as a data analyst inside a manufacturing organization, reporting directly to the Managing Director. That means the work spans the full stack of a decision: pulling from ERP extracts, cleaning and classifying data, building the model, and shaping the output into something an executive can act on in one read.

Outside that role, I'm moving deeper into applied ML and AI systems — retrieval-augmented generation, agent pipelines, and geospatial visualization — largely through self-directed side projects and a prior data science internship at a financial institution.

Work approach

Data doesn't help until it changes a decision

The same loop shows up in almost everything I build, from a one-off Excel model to a production pipeline.

Source

Start from the raw extract

Pull directly from ERP/SAP or another system of record, rather than a downstream report someone else already shaped.

Model

Clean, classify, transform

Handle outliers deliberately, adjust for seasonality where it's real, and keep every step formula-transparent.

Translate

Shape it for the reader

An executive gets one read that leads to a decision; an analyst gets the full working behind it.

Correct

Iterate on what's wrong

Prefer surgical fixes over rewrites — and a clean rebuild only when patching would hide the real problem.
